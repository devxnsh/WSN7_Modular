SECTION 2.1: INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

This chapter surveys the body of established knowledge that underpins the 
proposed secure, machine-learning-enforced architecture for Wireless Sensor 
Networks (WSNs).

The review is organized thematically to progress through:
- Structural and physical characteristics of WSNs
- Adversarial taxonomies
- Backhaul and radio architectures
- Cryptographic mechanisms
- Application of machine learning for intrusion detection and adaptive recovery

Each section concludes with explicit identification of limitations of prior work.

Section 2.9 synthesizes these limitations into a consolidated statement of 
research gaps that directly motivate the architectural contributions developed 
in Chapter 3.

LITERATURE SOURCES:
- Peer-reviewed journal articles
- Conference proceedings
- Technical standards documents
- Granted patents

All sources have been verified to the extent possible. Where source provenance 
could not be independently verified to the required exactness, the relevant 
citation is annotated as provisional in the reference list.

SECTION 2.2: WIRELESS SENSOR NETWORK FUNDAMENTALS
================================================================================

2.2.1 DEFINITION, ARCHITECTURE, AND STANDARDS

DEFINITION OF WSN:
- Spatially distributed collection of autonomous devices
- Each equipped with minimum set:
  * Sensing transducer
  * Microcontroller
  * Wireless radio
  * Power source
- Each node converts physical-world observable into digital measurement:
  * Temperature
  * Pressure
  * Motion
  * Ambient light
- Communicates measurement toward central data sink

DOMINANT SHORT-RANGE COMMUNICATION STANDARD:
- IEEE 802.15.4
- Specifies Physical (PHY) and Medium Access Control (MAC) layers
- For low-power, short-range communication
- Operates across twenty-seven ISM-band channels:
  * 868 MHz
  * 915 MHz
  * 2.4 GHz

ZIGBEE PROTOCOL STACK:
- Built atop IEEE 802.15.4
- Adds application-layer functionality:
  * AES-128 link encryption
  * Support for star, tree, and mesh network topologies

SENSOR NODE INTERNAL ARCHITECTURE:
- Reflects deliberate minimalism
- Components:
  * Small microcontroller handles sensing and control logic
  * Transceiver manages radio communication
  * Power management subsystem extends operational lifetime through aggressive 
    sleep scheduling
  * Optional flash memory permits local data buffering

CONSTRAINTS IMPOSED BY ARCHITECTURE:
- Hard limits on software complexity:
  * Inference models must be compact
  * Cryptographic overhead must be bounded
  * Radio communication must be infrequent

HIERARCHICAL STRUCTURING IN LARGE-SCALE DEPLOYMENTS:
- Flat topology typically hierarchically structured
- Includes Cluster Heads (CHs) and Gateway Nodes (GWNs)
- CHs and GWNs:
  * Aggregate sensor readings
  * Encrypt aggregated data
  * Relay aggregated readings toward sink

ALTERNATIVE STANDARD - IEEE 802.11ah (WI-FI HALOW):
- Extends Wi-Fi operation to sub-1 GHz band
- Offers improved penetration relative to 2.4 GHz IEEE 802.15.4
- Offers extended range
- Higher power profile and protocol complexity
- Most appropriate for gateway-tier nodes rather than leaf nodes

INDUSTRIAL STANDARDS:
- WirelessHART: Industrially oriented WSN standard
- ISA100.11a: Industrially oriented WSN standard
- Choice of physical/MAC layer standard must match:
  * Deployment context
  * Power budget

2.2.2 APPLICATION DOMAINS

ENVIRONMENTAL MONITORING:
- Air quality measurement
- Water quality surveillance
- Wildfire detection
- Seismic activity logging
- Instruments large and otherwise inaccessible terrain
- Network reliability and data integrity paramount

AGRICULTURAL DEPLOYMENTS:
- Precision irrigation
- Soil condition monitoring
- Crop health surveillance
- Node lifetimes must span entire growing seasons without intervention

INDUSTRIAL APPLICATIONS:
- Structural health monitoring of civil infrastructure:
  * Bridges
  * Pipelines
- Predictive maintenance in manufacturing facilities
- Supply-chain asset tracking

HEALTHCARE DEPLOYMENTS:
- Patient monitoring in hospital wards
- Process readings from wearable physiological sensors
- Smart-home monitoring for elderly populations
- Data confidentiality carries regulatory and clinical implications

DEFENCE AND BORDER-SURVEILLANCE (HIGHEST CRITICALITY):
- Perimeter monitoring
- Battlefield sensor grids
- Aerospace ground-support infrastructure
- Network compromise carries consequences far beyond data loss
- Extends to physical safety and national security

2.2.3 STRUCTURAL VULNERABILITIES

BROADCAST NATURE OF RF MEDIUM:
- Every transmission exposed to passive interception

NODES IN UNCONTROLLED/HOSTILE ENVIRONMENTS:
- Subject to hardware capture by adequately resourced adversaries

ENERGY AND MEMORY BUDGET CONSTRAINTS:
- Preclude classical cryptographic mechanisms:
  * Full Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) handshakes impractical
  * Frequent re-keying impractical
  * Computationally intensive authentication schemes impractical

MAC-LAYER PROTOCOL VULNERABILITIES:
- Token passing protocols introduce predictable timing patterns
- Duty cycling introduces predictable timing patterns
- Both exploitable by adversaries

CENTRALIZED ANOMALY DETECTION LIMITATIONS:
- Cannot respond within temporal bounds of fast-evolving attacks:
  * Flooding
  * Coordinated sinkhole formation

STRUCTURAL PROPERTIES DEFINE THREAT SURFACE:
- Distinguishes WSN threat profile from conventional networked systems
- Forms basis of attack taxonomy reviewed in following section

SECTION 2.3: ATTACK TAXONOMY FOR WIRELESS SENSOR NETWORKS
================================================================================

2.3.1 CLASSIFICATION FRAMEWORK

ORTHOGONAL CLASSIFICATION DIMENSIONS:

Dimension 1 - Protocol Layer:
- Physical layer (PHY)
- MAC layer (MAC)
- Network layer (NET)
- Cross-layer attacks

Dimension 2 - Adversarial Objective:
- Destructive attacks: Degrade or eliminate network functionality
- Extractive attacks: Operate covertly alongside normal network traffic

TAXONOMY ADAPTATION SOURCE:
- Multi-layer feature analysis on Kasasbeh WSN-DS dataset

2.3.2 DESTRUCTIVE ATTACKS

FLOODING ATTACKS:
- Saturate neighbouring radios with excessive packets
- Cause collisions
- Accelerate battery drain on surrounding nodes
- Mechanism: Packet injection to saturate radio queues
- Detection Signatures: MAC queue depth, retransmission count, PDR drop

BLACKHOLE ATTACK:
- Silently discards all packets routed through compromised node
- Leaves no statistical trail beyond throughput reduction
- Mechanism: Silent packet discard
- Detection Signatures: Token holding time, PDR, neighbor count

GRAYHOLE ATTACK:
- Selectively drops configurable fraction of packets
- Drops based on implied priority
- Statistically harder to distinguish from legitimate channel loss
- Mechanism: Selective, priority-weighted dropping
- Detection Signatures: PDR variance, BER, LQI correlation

SINKHOLE ATTACK:
- Exploits routing protocol
- Advertises falsely strong routes
- Attracts and then discards upstream traffic
- Corrupts topological map maintained by routing algorithms
- Mechanism: Advertises inflated route quality
- Detection Signatures: CH re-election frequency, hop-count distortion

DENIAL-OF-SLEEP ATTACK:
- Exploits knowledge of node duty cycle
- Injects wakeup messages immediately before node enters sleep state
- Drains battery reserves
- No immediately visible disruption to throughput
- Mechanism: Injects wakeup signals timed to node sleep transitions
- Detection Signatures: Residual energy asymmetry vs. duty cycle

WORMHOLE ATTACK:
- Tunnels packets between distant network regions
- Uses private out-of-band channel
- Creates phantom links
- Distorts topological view maintained by routing algorithms
- Mechanism: Tunnels packets via out-of-band channel
- Detection Signatures: Hop-count inconsistency, RSSI vs. distance mismatch

ATTACK COMBINATION DANGER:
- Attacks particularly dangerous when deployed in combination
- Dangerous when deployed at varying intensities
- Dangerous when statistically indistinguishable from stochastic channel 
  variation

2.3.3 EXTRACTIVE ATTACKS

EAVESDROPPING:
- Interception, duplication, or silent rerouting of received packets to 
  unintended destinations
- Without link-layer encryption, requires no modification of network behavior
- Hardest class to detect
- Mechanism: Passive interception
- Detection Signatures: Encrypted traffic analysis; hard to detect actively

SYBIL ATTACK:
- Fabricates multiple virtual node identities
- Poisons neighbor tables
- Subverts routing and cluster-formation decisions
- Mechanism: Fabricates multiple virtual node identities
- Detection Signatures: Neighbor count anomaly, cluster formation irregularity

MAN-IN-THE-MIDDLE ATTACK:
- Encompasses passive interception variant
- Does not require packet injection
- Can persist for extended periods before detection
- Mechanism: Intercepts and potentially modifies traffic between nodes
- Detection Signatures: Key validation failures, packet injection count

2.3.4 LIMITATIONS OF PRIOR ATTACK ANALYSIS

DESCRIPTIVE NATURE:
- Prior taxonomies largely descriptive
- Do not quantify detectability of each attack class relative to statistical 
  baseline of normal WSN operation

ISOLATION EVALUATION:
- Majority of intrusion detection studies evaluate attack classes in isolation
- Rather than as combinatorial deployments
- Understates difficulty of real-world detection

MISSING UNIFIED FRAMEWORK:
- Absence of unified, per-class behavioral signature framework
- Does not link physical observables to attack type
- Identified as gap addressed by feature engineering component of proposed 
  architecture

SECTION 2.4: BACKHAUL ARCHITECTURES
================================================================================

2.4.1 ROLE AND SIGNIFICANCE OF THE BACKHAUL LAYER

DEFINITION:
- Communications substrate carrying aggregated data from cluster-level 
  collection points upstream toward sink

FUNDAMENTAL GOVERNANCE:
- Fundamentally governs:
  * Latency
  * Reliability
  * Energy consumption
  * Adversarial resilience at network level

RESEARCH ATTENTION:
- Despite centrality, historically received less focused attention in WSN 
  security literature than leaf-node access tier

2.4.2 SINGLE-RADIO BACKHAUL AND ITS LIMITATIONS

IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH:
- In low-risk, short-range deployments, backhaul often implemented on same 
  IEEE 802.15.4 radio used for intra-cluster access

TRAFFIC CLASS CONFLATION:
- Single-radio approach conflates two traffic classes with fundamentally 
  distinct requirements:
  * Short-range, high-frequency sensor reporting
  * Long-distance, aggregated data forwarding

PERFORMANCE DEGRADATION:
- Contention between traffic classes degrades performance of both
- Compromised access-tier node gains direct visibility into backbone traffic

INTEGRATION STUDY (HASSAN ET AL.):
- Demonstrates performance gains when two traffic classes served on physically 
  distinct substrates
- Confirms integration of IEEE 802.15.4 sensor networks with wireless mesh 
  backhaul network
- Architecture limitation: Stops short of providing cryptographically 
  integrated, attack-resilient backhaul design

2.4.3 LORA AND LORAWAN

ADVANTAGES:
- Extends communication range to several kilometers
- Uses chirp spread-spectrum modulation
- Provides physically distinct long-range channel suitable for backbone 
  forwarding

CRITICAL LIMITATION:
- Strict duty-cycle constraints
- As low as one percent in certain regulatory regions
- Prevents application to real-time, event-driven WSN scenarios when used as 
  sole radio

RESOLUTION:
- Duty-cycle limitation cannot be resolved within single-radio architecture
- Necessitates complementary pairing with second radio

2.4.4 MAC AND ROUTING PROTOCOLS FOR THE BACKHAUL

TOKEN-PASSING MAC PROTOCOLS:
- Significant advantage: Eliminates MAC-layer flooding and collision attacks
- Grants transmission rights exclusively to valid token holder

HYBRID ZIGBEE–LORA ARCHITECTURE (LIU ET AL.):
- Employs token-ring protocol at ZigBee tier
- Employs polling mechanism at LoRa backbone tier
- Results:
  * Packet loss rates below one percent
  * Communication ranges exceeding 3.7 km
  * Performance unattainable with single-radio protocol under ISM-band 
    regulatory constraints

TOKEN-BASED SCHEME VULNERABILITIES:
- Replay attacks
- Token-theft vulnerabilities:
  * Compromised node retains or duplicates token
  * Starves legitimate nodes of transmission rights

LEACH PROTOCOL (LOW-ENERGY ADAPTIVE CLUSTERING HIERARCHY):
- Introduced probabilistic cluster-head rotation
- Balances energy load across network
- Issues:
  * Random election produces topologically suboptimal clusters
  * Well-documented vulnerability to CH-impersonation attacks:
    - Malicious node falsely claims CH status
    - Intercepts cluster traffic

TRANSPORT-LAYER MULTIPATH ROUTING (STEPHAN ET AL.):
- Provides resilience against single-path failure
- Requires state management at intermediate nodes
- Incompatible with memory constraints of low-power gateway hardware

PROPOSED ARCHITECTURE ALTERNATIVE:
- Addresses token-theft and replay vulnerabilities of prior MAC schemes
- Uses cryptographically bound alternating Tx-Rx chain
- Each gateway node's transmission phase encoded in final bit of locally 
  derived key
- Eliminates need for separate token management broadcast

2.4.5 LIMITATIONS OF PRIOR BACKHAUL WORK

CONSISTENT LIMITATION:
- Absence of integration between MAC-layer security mechanisms and network's 
  cryptographic key management system

INTEGRATION GAPS:
- Token-passing schemes do not cryptographically bind token to key hierarchy
- LoRaWAN architectures do not provide mechanisms for:
  * Detecting nodes violating duty-cycle constraints as attack strategy
  * Isolating nodes violating duty-cycle constraints

PROPOSED ARCHITECTURE ADDRESSES:
- Directly addresses both integration gaps

SECTION 2.5: ENCRYPTION STANDARDS AND KEY MANAGEMENT IN WSNS
================================================================================

2.5.1 CRYPTOGRAPHIC CONSTRAINTS AT THE SENSOR TIER

CRYPTOGRAPHY'S ROLE:
- Primary tool for ensuring:
  * Data confidentiality
  * Data integrity
  * Data authenticity

COST CONSTRAINTS:
- Classical cryptographic primitives impose energy and computational costs
- Costs scale poorly with resource constraints of leaf-node hardware

PKI HANDSHAKES:
- Require modular exponentiation or elliptic-curve point multiplication
- Energy cost can exceed that of transmitting hundreds of data packets
- Unworkable at leaf-node tier

DESIGN CHALLENGE:
- Not whether to use cryptography, but how to select, layer, and distribute 
  cryptographic responsibilities across tiers with radically different resource 
  profiles

2.5.2 SYMMETRIC ENCRYPTION AT THE SENSOR TIER

AES-128:
- Adopted by IEEE 802.15.4 for link-layer encryption
- 128-bit block cipher
- Key-schedule computational cost within reach of modern low-power 
  microcontrollers
- Operating at 16 to 32 MHz

SECURITY ASSESSMENT (SALEEM ET AL.):
- Confirms AES-128 in CCM mode provides adequate authentication and 
  confidentiality margins
- Threat models relevant to WSN deployment
- Caveat: Key management constitutes dominant attack surface rather than cipher

ENERGY EFFICIENCY (ZHANG ET AL.):
- Hardware-accelerated implementation on contemporary microcontrollers
- Achieves acceptable energy-per-bit figures
- For deployment scenarios with moderate transmission frequencies

LIGHTWEIGHT ALTERNATIVES:

HIGHT Cipher:
- 64-bit block cipher
- 128-bit key
- Uses Generalized Feistel Structure
- Proposed specifically for resource-constrained embedded environments
- Substantially lower energy consumption per encryption operation than AES-128
- Maintains adequate security margins against known attacks

CONSENSUS ASSESSMENT:
- AES-128 represents acceptable balance for most deployment scenarios
- Particularly where target microcontroller incorporates hardware-accelerated 
  AES engine

MESSAGE AUTHENTICATION CODE OVERHEAD:
- AES in CCM mode appends Message Authentication Code (MAC) to encrypted 
  payloads
- Power consumption and calculation requirements of AES in CCM mode for WSN/IoT 
  devices:
  * 128-bit MIC generation adds predictable, bounded overhead
  * Compatible with duty cycles of representative leaf-node platforms

ZIGBEE D2D MUTUAL AUTHENTICATION (ALLAKANY ET AL.):
- Confirms AES-128 with 128-bit MIC provides sufficient security
- Against replay attacks and injection attacks
- Within ZigBee threat model

2.5.3 KEY MANAGEMENT SCHEMES

SINGLE GLOBAL KEY VULNERABILITY:
- If single global network key distributed to all nodes
- Compromise of any one node exposes entire network's traffic to decryption

HIERARCHICAL KEY MANAGEMENT:
- Addresses single-key vulnerability
- Pre-distributes tier-specific pairwise keys
- Deletes global network key from individual nodes after initialization
- Localizes impact of single-node compromise to cluster where node resides

EAHKM+ SCHEME:
- Exemplifies hierarchical approach
- Incorporates rekeying mechanism
- Operates without requiring global keying material redistribution
- Reduces communication overhead associated with compromise recovery

SCALABILITY IN LARGE DEPLOYMENTS (ALSHAMMARI & ELLEITHY):
- Efficient and secure key distribution protocol
- Demonstrates pairwise key pre-distribution with cluster-localised rekeying
- Computationally feasible
- Resilient to node-capture attacks

IDENTITY-BASED AGGREGATE SIGNATURE SCHEMES:
- Reduce bandwidth cost of per-node authentication
- Allow cluster heads to aggregate signatures from multiple child nodes
- Single compact message sent before forwarding upstream

HYBRID KEY ESTABLISHMENT (INGALE & TANKKAR):
- Modified AODV protocol combined with:
  * Diffie-Hellman key exchange
  * Hash-based message authentication scheme
- Feasible in WSN contexts where gateway nodes have sufficient processing 
  resources
- Limitation: Does not address energy implications of Diffie-Hellman at 
  leaf-node tier

LIGHTWEIGHT SYMMETRIC CIPHERS PREFERRED:
- Consistently outperform public-key approaches on constrained hardware
- When throughput and energy jointly optimized
- Confirmed by Tawalbeh et al.

CHAOS-BASED ALTERNATIVE (BAI ET AL.):
- WSN message authentication code scheme based on chaos theory and XOR 
  encryption
- Suitable for nodes with insufficient resources for AES-128
- Limitation: Security margins against algebraic attacks not as thoroughly 
  peer-reviewed as standardized ciphers
- Proposed architecture opts for AES-128 in preference

2.5.4 PROPOSED LAYERED KEY ARCHITECTURE AND ITS DIFFERENTIATION

HIERARCHICAL KEY STRUCTURE:

Global Key:
- Held exclusively by sink
- Distributed through structured five-step locking handshake during network 
  initialization

Gateway Nodes:
- Derive local keys from global key

Cluster Heads:
- Receive only local key of parent gateway node

Leaf Sensor Nodes:
- Operate without cryptographic key material
- Transmit plaintext exclusively to cluster head
- Cluster head encrypts using both local and global keys before forwarding

DUAL-ENCRYPTION APPROACH:
- Confines any single-node breach to single cluster

CRYPTOGRAPHIC BINDING OF SCHEDULE:
- Final bit of each local key encodes gateway node's transmission phase
- Within alternating Tx-Rx chain
- Makes backhaul scheduling scheme cryptographically bound to key distribution 
  process
- Design property absent from all prior schemes reviewed

KEY VALIDATION:
- Failures at any tier immediately visible as trust score events
- Trigger daisy-chained polling mechanism

2.5.5 LIMITATIONS OF PRIOR CRYPTOGRAPHIC WORK

DOMINANT LIMITATION:
- Absence of integration between cryptographic layer and intrusion detection or 
  anomaly recovery system

TREATMENT OF COMPROMISE:
- Key management schemes treat compromise as event to be recovered from after 
  detection
- Do not specify the detection mechanism

PROPOSED ARCHITECTURE ADDRESSES:
- Makes key validation failures directly observable as trust score events
- Triggers daisy-chained polling mechanism described in Chapter 3

SECTION 2.6: DUAL-RADIO AND HETEROGENEOUS WSN ARCHITECTURES
================================================================================

2.6.1 MOTIVATION FOR DUAL-RADIO DESIGN

FUNDAMENTAL TRADE-OFF IN SINGLE-RADIO ARCHITECTURES:
- Physical properties enabling long-range communication:
  * Higher transmit power
  * Lower data rates
- In direct tension with energy efficiency required for:
  * Intra-cluster communication
  * High-frequency sensor reporting

RESOLUTION:
- Dual-radio architectures
- Equip gateway-tier nodes with two independent transceivers
- Each selected and configured for specific traffic class
- MAC layers entirely decoupled
- Contention in one channel does not degrade other

2.6.2 IEEE 802.15.4 / BLE AND LORA PAIRINGS

MOST EXTENSIVELY STUDIED COMBINATION:
- Short-range radio: IEEE 802.15.4 or Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)
- Long-range radio: LoRa or LoRaWAN

AYELE ET AL. WILDLIFE MONITORING DEPLOYMENT:
- BLE for data collection from proximate sensors
- LoRa for forwarding aggregated records to distant base station
- Confirmed results:
  * Two radios operate concurrently without mutual interference
  * LoRa duty-cycle constraint does not degrade access-tier performance
  * MAC layers entirely independent
  * Each radio's MAC optimized independently for its traffic class

STATHOPOULOS ET AL. STUDY:
- Validates dual-radio approach in context of maximizing network lifetime
- 3G gateway assignment
- Demonstrates routing framework for dual-radio sensor networks
- Shows that gateway-tier node selection and placement is itself optimization 
  problem with non-trivial solutions
- Establishes principle: Gateway-tier nodes must perform MAC bridging between 
  access and backbone domains
- Without introducing cross-tier interference
- Carried forward into proposed architecture

2.6.3 ZIGBEE–LORA HYBRID SYSTEMS

LIU ET AL. / TRUONG ET AL. SYSTEM:
- Combines two ZigBee sensor clusters and two LoRa sensor clusters
- Through ZigBee-to-LoRa converter nodes
- Managed by central LoRa gateway

MAC PROTOCOLS:
- Token-ring protocol governs ZigBee cluster access
- Polling mechanism applied to LoRa backbone

REPORTED RESULTS:
- Packet loss rates below one percent
- Backbone communication ranges exceeding 3.7 km
- Performance unattainable with single-radio protocol under ISM-band 
  regulatory constraints

VALIDATES PRINCIPLE:
- Hybrid MAC schemes feasible
- Two distinct traffic classes served simultaneously
- No mutual degradation
- Central design principle of proposed gateway-node architecture

TWONET LARGE-SCALE DUAL-RADIO TESTBED (LI ET AL.):
- Provides empirical evidence at scale
- Demonstrates dual-radio sensor nodes manufactured at:
  * Compatible densities
  * Compatible costs for large real-world deployments
- Testbed experience highlights:
  * Firmware complexity introduced by managing two independent radio stacks
  * Single microcontroller running both stacks
- Constraint informs RTOS-based gateway design in Chapter 3

2.6.4 GATEWAY NODE HARDWARE CONSTRAINTS

DEMANDS PLACED ON GATEWAY NODES:
- Substantially more demanding than leaf-node constraints
- Direct consequence of dual-radio requirement
- Must:
  * Manage two independent radio stacks
  * Run both access and backbone MAC protocols concurrently
  * Perform cryptographic operations on upstream data
  * Execute real-time machine learning inference
  * All within power budget supplemented by energy harvesting
  * Sustain for unattended operational lifetime

HARDWARE MANDATES:
- Processing or co-processing architecture capable of:
  * Preemptive multithreading via RTOS
- Microcontroller with:
  * Hardware-accelerated AES support
- Power system:
  * Large-capacity battery
  * Supplemented by photovoltaic or energy-harvesting circuitry
- Radio modules:
  * Dual-radio with high-gain external antennas
  * For backbone link

LEAF NODE REQUIREMENTS:
- Comprise majority of deployment
- No specialization required
- Any commercial low-power microcontroller with:
  * Adequate sleep-scheduling control
  * Sufficient memory for compressed local inference model
  * Sufficient memory for compressed local trust model

HETEROGENEOUS WSN DEPLOYMENT STRATEGIES (BOZKAYA ET AL.):
- Confirms distinction between gateway-tier and leaf-tier hardware
- Not merely optimization but structural requirement
- For achieving concurrent coverage and lifetime objectives
- In large-scale deployments
- Deployment modeling framework provides basis for gateway placement 
  optimization considerations

2.6.5 PATENT LITERATURE

WIRELESS CODED COMMUNICATION DEVICE ARCHITECTURE:
- US Patent 10,582,358 B1
- Describes dual-radio device architecture
- Heterogeneous backhaul and access traffic functions
- Corroborates commercial viability of hardware pairing
- Adopted in proposed framework

ATTACK PREVENTION ARCHITECTURE FOR IOT NETWORKS:
- US Patent 11,973,783 B1 (McLinden & Hartley)
- Proposes adaptive isolation mechanism for compromised nodes
- Provides conceptual parallel to daisy-chained polling and node-reset 
  mechanism in proposed architecture
- Limitation: Does not address WSN-specific hardware constraints
- Limitation: Does not provide empirical evaluation on WSN datasets

2.6.6 LIMITATIONS OF PRIOR DUAL-RADIO WORK

INTEGRATION GAP:
- No prior dual-radio WSN architecture integrates radio pairing with 
  cryptographic key hierarchy
- Key does not directly encode MAC-layer scheduling scheme

DESIGN SILOS:
- Existing architectures treat radio hardware, MAC protocol, and security layer 
  as independent design concerns
- Coordinated only at application layer

PROPOSED FRAMEWORK DISTINCTION:
- Cryptographic binding of transmission schedule to key distribution process
- As detailed in Chapter 3

SECTION 2.7: MACHINE LEARNING FOR ATTACK DETECTION AND PREVENTION
================================================================================

2.7.1 NECESSITY OF MACHINE LEARNING IN WSN INTRUSION DETECTION

LIMITATIONS OF RULE-BASED SYSTEMS:
- Fixed threshold monitors cannot adapt to:
  * Stochastic variation of attack intensities
  * Gradual drift of node behavior over time
  * Combinatorial diversity of multi-attack scenarios

BEHAVIORAL SIGNATURE MANIFESTATION:
- WSN attack signatures manifest across multiple protocol layers simultaneously:
  * Flooding: Elevates MAC-layer queue depth, increases retransmissions
  * Sinkhole: Suppresses CH re-election frequency, distorts hop-count distances
  * Denial-of-Sleep: Produces residual energy asymmetry inconsistent with node's 
    reported duty cycle

MULTI-LAYER PATTERN RECOGNITION:
- Multi-layer, correlated signatures exactly what supervised classifiers excel 
  at learning
- Requires sufficiently rich feature vector from labeled historical data

2.7.2 FEATURE ENGINEERING FOR WSN INTRUSION DETECTION

FEATURE ORGANIZATION:
- Four protocol-layer groups
- Cataloged in Kasasbeh WSN-DS dataset
- Total: 23-feature schema

PHYSICAL LAYER PARAMETERS:
- Received signal strength (RSSI)
- Link quality indicator (LQI)
- Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)
- Bit error rate (BER)
- Packet error rate (PER)
- Characterize channel fidelity and signal degradation

MAC LAYER PARAMETERS:
- Packet delivery ratio (PDR)
- End-to-end latency
- Queue depth
- Duty cycle
- Token holding time
- Expose medium-access efficiency anomalies

NETWORK LAYER PARAMETERS:
- Hop count
- Neighbor count
- Residual energy
- CH re-election frequency
- Retransmission counts
- Indicate topological health and energy depletion patterns

SECURITY LAYER PARAMETERS:
- Key overhead
- Rekeying frequency
- Intrusion event rate
- Packet injection count
- Quantify adversarial interaction and network stress

SIGNATURE MAPPING FROM KASASBEH DATASET:
- RSSI drops: Indicate jamming
- Anomalous token holding time: Exposes Blackhole nodes
- CH re-election spikes: Characteristic of Sinkhole attacks advertising false 
  routes
- Residual energy asymmetry across nodes: Fingerprint of Denial-of-Sleep 
  exploitation

EMBEDDED INFERENCE CONSTRAINT:
- Full 23-feature vector infeasible for real-time inference on leaf-tier 
  microcontrollers
- Operating at 16 to 32 MHz with kilobyte-scale RAM

COMPRESSED FEATURE SUBSET SOLUTION:
- Captures both channel state and behavioral anomaly
- Negligible runtime overhead
- Maximizes discriminative power per computation cycle

LEAF-TIER FEATURE SUBSET:
- RSSI
- PDR
- Retransmission count
- Residual energy
- Token holding time
- Queue depth
- Approximates full-feature model performance on dominant attack classes
- Fits within memory and compute constraints of:
  * ARM Cortex-M0 devices
  * ARM Cortex-M4 devices

EMBEDDED ML OPTIMIZATION TECHNIQUES:
- Network pruning
- Fixed-point quantization
- Decision tree compression
- Reduce memory footprint to levels compatible with:
  * 2 to 32 KB SRAM available on representative leaf-node microcontrollers

2.7.3 CLASSIFIER SELECTION AND PERFORMANCE ON WSN-DS

DECISION TREE CLASSIFIER (DTC):
- Overall accuracy: 99.48%

RANDOM FOREST CLASSIFIER (RFC):
- Overall accuracy: 99.68%

RFC SELECTION RATIONALE:
- Ensemble of 100 trees
- Eliminates instability of individual decision trees
- Particularly important under severe class imbalance in dataset:
  * Normal traffic exceeds 90% of samples
  * Flooding minority ratio exceeds 100:1
- Internal feature importance scores provide interpretable audit trail
- Unavailable from single-tree classifiers without restrictive depth limits

RFC FEATURE IMPORTANCE ANALYSIS:

Ranking (by discriminative power):
1. ADV_S (advertisement send count): 18.3%
2. Expended energy: 14.8%
3. Is_CH flag: 12.9%
4. Data sent to base station: 10.2%
5. SCH_S (scheduling counter): 8.0%

KEY FINDING:
- Attack classes most distinguishable at control-plane level
- Not through raw data throughput

PER-CLASS ACCURACIES:
- Blackhole: 99.4%
- Flooding: 98.1%
- Grayhole: 98.7%
- Normal: 99.9%
- Scheduling Attack: 93.6%

SCHEDULING ATTACK ACCURACY:
- Lower than other classes (93.6%)
- Attributable to statistical similarity to normal duty-cycle variation
- Motivates inclusion of cryptographically bound scheduling metadata in feature 
  vector

2.7.4 EMBEDDED MACHINE LEARNING ON CONSTRAINED HARDWARE

OPTIMIZATION TECHNIQUES (BRANCO ET AL. SURVEY):
- Network pruning
- Fixed-point quantization
- Decision tree compression
- Reduce model memory footprint to 2-32 KB SRAM levels
- Available on representative leaf-node microcontrollers

QUANTIZATION PRESERVATION:
- Post-training quantization of tree-based ensembles to 8-bit fixed-point
- Preserves classification accuracy within one percentage point
- Of floating-point baseline
- For structured tabular datasets generated by WSN monitoring
- Directly applicable to compressed local model in proposed architecture

FPGA CO-PROCESSORS:
- Highest-throughput option for embedded inference
- Where silicon area and power budget permit
- Identified by broader ML survey for embedded and mobile devices

FPGA IMPLEMENTATIONS:
- Vibration analysis and fault diagnosis in wireless sensor nodes
- ADALINE neural network co-processor
- Integrated into gateway-tier node without exceeding power budget
- Confirms hardware-accelerated inference feasible at gateway tier

NEURAL NETWORK ARCHITECTURES IN FPGA:
- Kareem et al.: FPGA implementations for WSN fault detection
- Hadi et al.: FPGA implementations for fault detection in smart WSNs
- Anand & Keetha Manjari: FPGA-based ANN for forest fire detection in WSN
- All corroborate viability of hardware-accelerated inference for gateway-tier 
  nodes

2.7.5 WIRELESS ACOUSTIC SENSOR NETWORKS AND EDGE COMPUTING

EDGE COMPUTING APPLICATION:
- Wireless acoustic sensor networks (Sheng et al.)
- Demonstrates rapid acoustic monitoring tasks requiring low-latency inference
- Distributed across network tiers
- Directly analogous to tiered inference architecture proposed

MESHED ARCHITECTURE:
- Edge-computing meshed WASN architecture (Caro-Via et al.)
- Confirms computational division between:
  * Constrained edge nodes
  * More capable gateway or sink tiers
- Both architecturally sound and practically validated in deployed systems

2.7.6 LIMITATIONS OF PRIOR MACHINE LEARNING WORK IN WSNS

STATIC MODELS:
- Prior approaches deploy static, training-time-frozen models
- Deployed at network edge
- Offer no mechanism for in-service adaptation
- As network's normal operating statistics evolve with:
  * Node aging
  * Environmental change
  * Topology adaptation

SINGLE-TIER EVALUATION:
- Majority of prior work evaluates classifiers exclusively at sink level
- Provides:
  * Global visibility
  * High latency
- Or evaluated at node tier
- Provides:
  * Low latency
  * Limited discriminative power
- No coordinated dual-tier system with explicit handoff

RECALIBRATION LOOP GAP:
- Absence of continuous recalibration loop
- No mechanism for global model at sink to push updated local decision 
  boundaries to constrained nodes over backbone channel
- Most significant gap in existing literature

PROPOSED ARCHITECTURE ADDRESSES:
- Dual-inference and recalibration architecture described in Chapter 3

SECTION 2.8: TRUST MANAGEMENT AND ADAPTIVE RECOVERY
================================================================================

TRUST MANAGEMENT FRAMEWORKS:
- Assign dynamic trust score to each node
- Based on observed behavioral metrics
- Trigger isolation or interrogation procedures
- When node's score falls below threshold

MCLINDEN & HARTLEY FRAMEWORK:
- Proposes adaptive isolation of compromised IoT nodes
- Does not specify mechanism for trust score propagation
- In hierarchically tiered WSN
- Does not address latency constraints of real-time attack response

BROADER WSN LITERATURE:
- Daousis et al. identify trust management as open problem
- Note that most published schemes require inter-node communication
- For trust updates
- Inter-node communication itself vulnerable to:
  * Sybil attacks
  * Man-in-the-Middle attacks
- That scheme designed to detect

CIRCULAR DEPENDENCY:
- Most existing schemes exhibit circularity
- Communication channel used for trust updates itself vulnerable to detected 
  attacks

PROPOSED ARCHITECTURE RESOLUTION:
- Anchors trust score updates to observable cryptographic events
- Specifically: Key validation failures at any tier
- Detectable locally without requiring inter-node trust communication
- Key validation failures trigger daisy-chained polling mechanism
- Isolates and resets suspect nodes before significant data loss accumulates

GLOBAL MODEL FUNCTION:
- RFC model at sink provides longer-term recalibration
- Distinguishes genuine compromise from transient environmental effects

INTEGRATION:
- Integration of cryptographic observability with machine-learning-based 
  recovery
- Constitutes primary architectural novelty
- Relative to all prior work reviewed

SECTION 2.9: RESEARCH GAPS AND LIMITATIONS OF PRIOR WORK
================================================================================

PATTERN IN EXISTING ARCHITECTURES:
- Existing WSN security architectures address individual design dimensions in 
  isolation
- Cryptographic schemes do not integrate with intrusion detection
- Intrusion detection schemes do not integrate with key management or recovery 
  mechanisms
- Dual-radio architectures do not integrate with cryptographic layer
- Embedded machine learning deployments do not incorporate continuous 
  recalibration loops

IDENTIFIED RESEARCH GAPS:

GAP 1 — ABSENCE OF CROSS-LAYER INTEGRATION:
- No prior architecture cryptographically binds MAC-layer scheduling scheme to 
  key distribution process
- Two subsystems coordinated only at application layer
- Therefore independently vulnerable

GAP 2 — STATIC EMBEDDED CLASSIFIERS:
- All prior embedded machine learning deployments reviewed freeze model 
  parameters at training time
- No mechanism exists for pushing updated decision boundaries from:
  * Global sink-level model
  * To constrained leaf-tier nodes
  * In service

GAP 3 — NO UNIFIED DUAL-TIER INFERENCE:
- Prior intrusion detection work deploys classifiers either:
  * At sink: Global visibility, high latency
  * At node: Low latency, limited discriminative power
- Not as coordinated dual-tier system
- Not with explicit handoff between local flagging and global recalibration

GAP 4 — TRUST WITHOUT CRYPTOGRAPHIC ANCHORING:
- Existing trust management frameworks rely on inter-node communication
- For trust score updates
- Introduces circular dependency
- Communication channel for trust updates vulnerable to detected attacks

GAP 5 — DEPLOYMENT REALISM OF HARDWARE CONSTRAINTS:
- Published evaluation results for embedded WSN classifiers predominantly from:
  * Simulation platforms
  * Laboratory testbeds
- Do not reflect concurrent computational load of dual-radio gateway node running:
  * RTOS
  * Two MAC stacks
  * AES encryption
  * Real-time inference
- Simultaneously

GAP 6 — PER-CLASS SCHEDULING ATTACK DETECTION:
- Scheduling Attack class achieves lowest per-class accuracy (93.6%)
- Across all evaluated classifiers on WSN-DS
- Reflects absence of cryptographic metadata in feature vector
- Incorporating key-bound scheduling information identified as means of:
  * Improving discriminative power for this class
