CHAPTER 2: LITERATURE REVIEW - CONTENT POINTS EXTRACTED

================================================================================
CHAPTER INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

This chapter surveys the foundational body of knowledge underlying the proposed 
secure, machine-learning-enforced architecture for Wireless Sensor Networks. The 
review is organized thematically across the structural and adversarial dimensions 
of WSN design. It opens with an overview of WSN fundamentals, canonical application 
domains, and the principal classes of vulnerability that motivate the proposed 
framework. Subsequent sections address the backhaul architectures that constitute 
the communications substrate, encryption standards viable within the low-power 
constraints of sensor hardware, dual-radio architectural patterns that resolve the 
range-versus-power trade-off, and the application of machine learning to attack 
detection and mitigation.

================================================================================
BASICS OF WIRELESS SENSOR NETWORKS
================================================================================

FUNDAMENTAL DEFINITION AND STANDARDS:
- A Wireless Sensor Network (WSN) is a spatially distributed collection of 
  autonomous devices, each equipped at minimum with:
  * Sensing transducer
  * Microcontroller
  * Wireless radio
  * Power source
- Each node converts a physical-world observable (temperature, pressure, motion, 
  ambient light) into a digital measurement and communicates it toward a sink
- IEEE 802.15.4 is the operating standard for low-power, short-range WSN 
  communication
- IEEE 802.15.4 specifies Physical and MAC layers across 27 ISM-band channels:
  * 868 MHz
  * 915 MHz
  * 2.4 GHz
- ZigBee is the application-layer stack built on IEEE 802.15.4
  * Provides AES-128 link encryption
  * Supports star, tree, and mesh topologies

SENSOR NODE ARCHITECTURE:
- Reflects deliberate minimalism:
  * Small microcontroller handles sensing and control logic
  * Transceiver manages radio communication
  * Power management subsystem extends battery life through aggressive sleep 
    scheduling
  * Optional flash memory enables local data buffering
- Hard constraints on all aspects of software design:
  * Models must be compact
  * Cryptographic overhead must be bounded
  * Communications must be infrequent
- Large-scale deployments organize flat topology into hierarchical structure:
  * Cluster Heads (CHs)
  * Gateway Nodes (GWNs)
  * These aggregate, encrypt, and relay sensor readings upstream toward sink

WSN APPLICATION DOMAINS:
- Environmental Monitoring:
  * Air quality
  * Water quality
  * Wildfire detection
  * Seismic activity
  * Instruments large, otherwise inaccessible terrain

- Agricultural Deployments:
  * Precision irrigation
  * Soil condition monitoring
  * Crop health surveillance

- Industrial Applications:
  * Structural health monitoring (bridges, pipelines)
  * Predictive maintenance in manufacturing
  * Supply-chain asset tracking

- Healthcare Applications:
  * Patient monitoring in hospital wards
  * Wearable physiological sensors
  * Smart-home monitoring for elderly populations

- Defense and Border-Surveillance (High Criticality):
  * Perimeter monitoring
  * Battlefield sensor grids
  * Aerospace ground-support infrastructure
  * Network compromise carries consequences far beyond data loss

STRUCTURAL VULNERABILITIES OF WSNs:
- Broadcast nature of RF medium exposes every transmission to passive interception
- Nodes physically deployed in uncontrolled or hostile environments make hardware 
  capture trivially executable by adequately resourced adversaries
- Energy and memory budgets preclude classical cryptographic mechanisms:
  * Full PKI handshakes impractical
  * Frequent re-keying impractical
  * Computationally intensive authentication schemes impractical
- MAC-layer protocols using token passing or duty cycling introduce predictable 
  timing patterns exploitable by adversaries
- Centralized anomaly detection cannot respond within temporal bounds of 
  fast-evolving attacks:
  * Flooding
  * Coordinated sinkhole formation

ATTACK CLASSIFICATION - DESTRUCTIVE VS. EXTRACTIVE:

DESTRUCTIVE ATTACKS (Degrade or eliminate network functionality):
- Flooding:
  * Saturates neighboring radios with excessive packets
  * Causes collisions
  * Accelerates battery drain on surrounding nodes

- Blackhole:
  * Silently discards all packets routed through it
  * Leaves no statistical trail

- Grayhole:
  * Selectively drops configurable fraction based on implied packet priority
  * Statistically harder to distinguish from legitimate channel loss

- Sinkhole:
  * Advertises falsely strong routes
  * Attracts then discards upstream traffic
  * Corrupts routing topology

- Denial-of-Sleep:
  * Exploits knowledge of node duty cycles
  * Injects wakeup messages immediately before node enters sleep state
  * Drains battery reserves without causing immediately visible disruption

- Wormhole:
  * Tunnels packets between distant network regions through private out-of-band 
    channel
  * Creates phantom links
  * Distorts topological map maintained by network

EXTRACTIVE ATTACKS (Operate without disrupting normal data flow):
- Eavesdropping:
  * Intercepts packets
  * Duplicates packets
  * Silently reroutes received packets to unintended destinations

- Sybil:
  * Fabricates multiple virtual identities
  * Poisons neighbor tables
  * Subverts routing and cluster-formation decisions

- Man-in-the-Middle:
  * Passive interception variant

ATTACK DEPLOYMENT CHARACTERISTICS:
- Attacks may be deployed in combination
- Attacks operate at varying intensities
- Attacks exhibit varying detectability
- Most dangerous variant: activity statistically indistinguishable from stochastic 
  channel variation

BACKHAUL ARCHITECTURES
================================================================================

DEFINITION AND ROLE:
- Backhaul refers to communication substrate carrying aggregated data from 
  cluster-level collection points upstream toward sink
- Fundamentally governs:
  * Latency
  * Reliability
  * Energy consumption
  * Adversarial resilience of entire network

SINGLE-RADIO BACKHAUL APPROACH:
- In low-risk, short-range deployments, backhaul often implemented on same 
  IEEE 802.15.4 radio used for intra-cluster access
- Single-radio approach creates problems:
  * Conflates two traffic classes with distinct requirements:
    - Short-range, high-frequency sensor reporting
    - Long-distance, aggregated data forwarding
  * Contention between classes degrades performance of both
  * Compromised access-tier node can directly affect backbone traffic

IEEE 802.15.4 AND ZIGBEE LIMITATIONS:
- Dominant short-range WSN substrate
- Supports mesh routing and AES-128 link-layer encryption
- Provides three-device model: Coordinators, Routers, End Devices
- Limitations:
  * Static cluster formation
  * Limited heterogeneous interoperability
  * Exposes to topology disruption attacks in dense/adversarially contested 
    environments
  * Absence of distinct backhaul channel means flooding/Sinkhole against Router 
    degrades both access and backbone simultaneously

LORA AND LORAWAN LIMITATIONS:
- Extends communication range to several kilometers
- Provides physically distinct long-range channel suitable for backbone
- Critical limitation: Strict duty-cycle constraints
  * As low as 1% in some regulatory regions
  * Prevents application to real-time, event-driven WSN scenarios
- Trade-off: Cannot resolve range-versus-duty-cycle within single-radio 
  architecture

HETEROGENEOUS DUAL-RADIO RESOLUTION:
- Addresses fundamental range/duty-cycle trade-off
- Gateway-tier nodes equipped with two independent transceivers operating on 
  distinct frequency bands
- One radio (short-range):
  * Typical: IEEE 802.15.4 or BLE interface
  * Handles intra-cluster traffic
- Second radio (long-range):
  * Most common: LoRa or LoRaWAN module
  * Manages backbone communication between cluster heads and sink
- Demonstrated success (Ayele et al.):
  * BLE and LoRa pairing for wildlife monitoring
  * Meets both short-range high-frequency requirements and long-distance 
    forwarding requirements
  * No duty-cycle violations with independent MAC layers
  * Each radio's MAC optimized independently
- Eliminates cross-tier interference
- Allows independent MAC optimization

BACKHAUL ROUTING AND MAC PROTOCOLS:

Token-Passing MAC:
- Example: Multi-Token MAC-cum-Routing Protocol
- Benefits:
  * Eliminates MAC-layer flooding and collision attacks
  * Grants transmission rights exclusively to valid token holder
  * Collision-free parallel transmissions from immediate parent nodes
- Vulnerabilities:
  * Replay attacks
  * Token theft
  * Compromised node retains/duplicates token to starve legitimate nodes

Hierarchical Routing:
- Example: LEACH (Low-Energy Adaptive Clustering Hierarchy)
- Introduced probabilistic cluster-head rotation to balance energy load
- Issues:
  * Random election produces topologically suboptimal clusters
  * Vulnerable to CH-impersonation attacks

Proposed Alternating Tx-Rx Chain:
- Uses local key's final bit to calculate gateway node's transmission phase
- Creates alternating Tx-Rx chain across gateway nodes
- Benefits:
  * Resolves backpressure naturally without explicit token management
  * Eliminates replay vulnerabilities of token-based schemes

ENCRYPTION IN WSN
================================================================================

CRYPTOGRAPHY'S ROLE AND CHALLENGE:
- Primary tool for ensuring data:
  * Confidentiality
  * Integrity
  * Authenticity
- Challenge: Classical cryptographic primitives impose energy and computational 
  costs that scale poorly with resource constraints
- Public-Key Infrastructure (PKI) handshakes unworkable at leaf-node level:
  * Require modular exponentiation or elliptic-curve point multiplication
  * Energy cost can exceed that of transmitting hundreds of data packets
- Tension: Not whether to use cryptography, but how to select, layer, and 
  distribute cryptographic responsibilities across tiers with radically different 
  resource profiles

SYMMETRIC KEY CRYPTOGRAPHY AT SENSOR LEVEL:
- Primary viable option at sensor level
- AES-128:
  * Adopted by IEEE 802.15.4 for link-layer encryption
  * Provides 128-bit block cipher
  * Key schedule computational cost well within reach of modern low-power 
    microcontrollers (16–32 MHz)
- Lightweight block ciphers (HIGHT):
  * Operates on 64-bit block with 128-bit key using GFS (Generalized Feistel 
    Structure)
  * Proposed specifically for resource-constrained embedded environments
  * Achieves substantially lower energy consumption per encryption operation than 
    AES-128
  * Maintains adequate security margins against known attacks
- Trade-off consensus: AES-128 represents acceptable balance for most deployment 
  scenarios, particularly with hardware-accelerated AES engines

KEY MANAGEMENT:
- Challenge: If single global network key distributed to all nodes, compromise 
  of any single node exposes entire network's traffic to decryption
- Hierarchical Key Management:
  * Pre-distribute tier-specific pairwise keys
  * Delete global network key from nodes after initialization
  * Localizes impact of single-node compromise to cluster where node resides
  * Example: EAHKM+ scheme with hierarchically partitioned pairwise keys and 
    rekeying mechanism operating without global keying material re-distribution
- ID-based Aggregate Signature Schemes:
  * Reduce bandwidth cost of per-node authentication
  * Allow cluster heads to aggregate signatures from multiple child nodes into 
    single compact message before forwarding

PROPOSED LAYERED KEY STRUCTURE:
- Global key:
  * Held only by sink
  * Distributed through structured five-step locking handshake during network 
    setup
- Gateway nodes:
  * Derive local keys from global key
- Cluster heads:
  * Receive only local key of their parent gateway node
- Leaf sensor nodes:
  * Operate without cryptographic key material
  * Transmit plaintext to cluster head
  * Cluster head encrypts using both local and global keys before forwarding
- Dual-encryption benefit: Confines any breach to single cluster for any single 
  compromised node
- Local key final bit repurposing:
  * Encodes node's transmission phase in alternating Tx-Rx chain
  * Eliminates need for separate scheduling broadcast
  * Makes backhaul phase schedule cryptographically bound to key distribution
- Key validation:
  * Failures at any tier immediately visible as trust score events
  * Trigger daisy-chained polling mechanism

================================================================================
DUAL-RADIO ARCHITECTURES
================================================================================

FUNDAMENTAL DESIGN TRADE-OFF:
- Single-radio WSN architectures face fundamental trade-off:
  * Properties enabling long-range communication (higher transmit power, lower 
    data rates) in direct tension with:
  * Energy efficiency required for intra-cluster, high-frequency sensor reporting
- Long-range optimization incurs prohibitive energy expenditure or unacceptable 
  duty-cycle constraints
- Dual-radio resolution:
  * Equip gateway-tier or cluster-head-tier nodes with two independent 
    transceivers
  * Each selected and configured for specific traffic class
  * Decouple MAC layers entirely
  * Contention in one channel does not degrade other

EXTENSIVELY STUDIED RADIO PAIRINGS:

BLE/IEEE 802.15.4 + LoRa/LoRaWAN:
- Short-range: BLE or IEEE 802.15.4 for intra-cluster access
- Long-range: LoRa or LoRaWAN for backbone forwarding
- Example: Ayele et al. wildlife monitoring
  * BLE for data collection from nearby sensors
  * LoRa for forwarding aggregated records to distant base station
  * Two radios operate concurrently without mutual interference
  * LoRa duty-cycle constraint did not degrade access-tier performance
  * MAC layers entirely independent
- Patent reference: US10582358B1 describes wireless coded communication (WCC) 
  device architecture with dual-radio functions for heterogeneous backhaul and 
  access traffic

HETEROGENEOUS WSN STUDY (Liu et al.):
- ZigBee–LoRa hybrid communication system
- Combined:
  * Two ZigBee sensor clusters
  * Two LoRa sensor clusters
  * Through ZigBee-to-LoRa converter nodes managed by central LoRa gateway
- Token ring protocol governed ZigBee cluster access
- Polling mechanism applied to LoRa backbone
- Results:
  * Packet loss rates below 1%
  * Communication ranges exceeding 3.7 km on backbone channel
  * Performance unattainable with any single-radio protocol within ISM-band 
    regulatory constraints
- Demonstrates:
  * Hybrid MAC architectures feasible
  * Two traffic classes can be served simultaneously with distinct MAC protocols
  * No mutual degradation
  * Central design principle of proposed framework's gateway-node architecture

GATEWAY NODE HARDWARE CONSTRAINTS:
- Substantially more demanding than leaf-node constraints
- Direct consequence of dual-radio requirement
- Demands:
  * Manage two independent radio stacks
  * Run both access and backbone MAC protocols concurrently
  * Perform cryptographic operations on upstream data
  * Execute real-time machine learning inference
  * All within power budget supplemented by energy harvesting
- Must sustain for unattended operational lifetime
- Hardware mandates:
  * Processing or co-processing architecture capable of preemptive multithreading 
    via RTOS
  * Microcontroller with hardware-accelerated AES support
  * Large-capacity battery supplemented by photovoltaic or energy-harvesting 
    circuit
  * Dual-radio modules with high-gain external antennas for backbone link
- Leaf nodes (majority of deployment):
  * No such specialization required
  * Any commercial low-power microcontroller with adequate sleep-scheduling 
    control and memory for compressed local trust model suffices

MACHINE LEARNING FOR ATTACK DETECTION AND PREVENTION
================================================================================

ML NECESSITY FOR WSN INTRUSION DETECTION:
- Rule-based systems and threshold monitors cannot adapt to:
  * Stochastic variation of attack intensities
  * Gradual drift of node behavior over time
  * Combinatorial diversity of multi-attack scenarios
- WSN attack behavioral signatures manifest across multiple protocol layers:
  * Flooding: Elevates MAC-layer queue depth, increases retransmissions, drains 
    neighboring node batteries
  * Sinkhole: Suppresses CH re-election frequency, distorts perceived hop-count 
    distances
  * Denial-of-Sleep: Produces residual energy asymmetry inconsistent with node's 
    reported duty cycle
- Multi-layer, correlated signatures are precisely what supervised classifiers 
  excel at learning
- Requires sufficiently rich feature vector from labeled historical data

MULTI-LAYER FEATURE SET FOR ATTACK CLASSIFICATION:

Physical Layer Parameters:
- Received signal strength (RSSI)
- Link quality indicator (LQI)
- Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)
- Bit error rate (BER)
- Packet error rate (PER)
- Characterize channel fidelity and signal degradation

MAC Layer Parameters:
- Packet delivery ratio (PDR)
- End-to-end latency
- Queue depth
- Duty cycle
- Token holding time
- Expose medium-access efficiency anomalies

Network Layer Parameters:
- Hop count
- Neighbor count
- Residual energy
- CH re-election frequency
- Retransmission counts
- Indicate topological health and energy depletion patterns

Security Layer Parameters:
- Key overhead
- Rekeying frequency
- Intrusion event rate
- Packet injection count
- Quantify adversarial interaction and network stress

ATTACK SIGNATURE MAPPING:
- RSSI drops: Indicate jamming
- Anomalous token holding time: Exposes Blackhole nodes
- CH re-election spikes: Characteristic of Sinkhole attacks advertising false 
  routes
- Residual energy asymmetry across nodes: Fingerprint of Denial-of-Sleep 
  exploitation

EMBEDDED ML PRACTICAL CONSTRAINT:
- Full 23-feature vector infeasible for real-time inference on leaf-tier 
  microcontrollers
- Operating at 16–32 MHz with kilobyte-scale RAM
- Solution: Compressed feature subset
  * Captures both channel state and behavioral anomaly
  * Negligible runtime overhead
  * Maximizes discriminative power per computation cycle

LEAF-TIER FEATURE SUBSET:
- Compressed features for constrained devices:
  * RSSI
  * PDR
  * Retransmission count
  * Residual energy
  * Token holding time
  * Queue depth
- Approximates classification performance of full-feature model on dominant 
  attack classes
- Fits within memory and compute constraints of typical ARM Cortex-M0 or M4 
  devices

EMBEDDED ML OPTIMIZATION TECHNIQUES:
- Network pruning
- Fixed-point quantization
- Decision tree compression
- Reduce memory footprint of inference model
- Achieve compatibility with 2–32 KB SRAM available on representative leaf-node 
  MCUs

SINK-LEVEL INFERENCE TIER:
- Compute constraint lifted at global sink-level
- Richer models become appropriate
- Evaluated on Kasasbeh WSN-DS dataset
- Dataset logs 23 per-node operational parameters across five behavioral classes:
  * Normal
  * Blackhole
  * Grayhole
  * Flooding
  * Scheduling Attack

MODEL PERFORMANCE:

Decision Tree Classifier (DTC):
- Overall accuracy: 99.48%

Random Forest Classifier (RFC):
- Overall accuracy: 99.68%

RFC Selected Because:
- Ensemble of 100 trees eliminates instability of individual trees
- Particularly important under severe class imbalance in dataset
  * Normal traffic exceeded 90% of samples
  * Flooding minority ratio exceeded 100:1
- Internal feature importance scores provide interpretable audit trail unavailable 
  from single-tree classifiers

RFC FEATURE IMPORTANCE ANALYSIS:
- ADV_S (advertisement send count): 18.3% (dominant discriminator)
- Expended Energy: 14.8%
- Is_CH flag: 12.9%
- Data_Sent_To_BS: 10.2%
- SCH_S: 8.0%
- Confirms attack classes most distinguishable at control-plane level
- Not through raw data throughput

RFC PER-CLASS ACCURACY:
- Blackhole: 99.4%
- Flooding: 98.1%
- Grayhole: 98.7%
- Normal: 99.9%
- Scheduling: 93.6%

DUAL-INFERENCE STRATEGY:
- Lightweight local model at node level
- Comprehensive global model at sink
- Addresses temporal limitations of centralized detection
- Retains statistical power of global network visibility

LOCAL MODEL FUNCTION:
- Provides rapid anomaly flagging within communication round during which attack 
  occurs
- Enables daisy-chained polling mechanism
- Isolates and resets suspect nodes before significant data loss accumulates

GLOBAL MODEL FUNCTION:
- Continuously updated and recalibrated by sink
- Addresses long-term drift afflicting static embedded classifier
- Network's normal operating statistics evolve over time with:
  * Node aging
  * Environmental change
  * Topology adaptation

CONTINUOUS RECALIBRATION LOOP:
- Global model pushes updated local decision boundaries to constrained nodes over 
  backbone channel
- Critical differentiator from prior work deploying static, training-time-frozen 
  models at edge
- Prior work offers no mechanism for in-service adaptation
